# LLM Council Transcript — MJ Recruit play, pre-Friday pitch
14 July 2026

## Original question
"Council everything": the live site + run-sheet app, the three-tier pricing (£199+£35 / £299+£69 / £399+£129), the midnight-rule/no-show pitch, the growth plan, and Paul's positioning as builder-insider asking for a paid ongoing deal, ahead of Friday's Zoom with MJ's owner.

## Framed question
Paul (Leicester, 3–4 years as casual worker + pickup driver for MJ Recruit, a small hospitality staffing agency supplying crews to event caterers D&J Catering and Sanjay's) pitches MJ's owner on Zoom this Friday. MJ has no web presence; ops run on WhatsApp via coordinator Dee; late cancellations cost £50–100 per staff member (MJ's own numbers). Paul has already built and deployed free: a marketing site (forms hand off to WhatsApp), a run-sheet web app for Dee (auto-plans pickup cars/times, tracks shifts per person, generates Dee's exact WhatsApp message; data in localStorage on one phone), and a pitch page with sourced stats, a growth plan (~300+ Midlands caterers target list), a "midnight rule" for no-shows (no confirmation by midnight → auto-text next standby; points against confirm-then-cancellers), and three subscription tiers: Site £199+£35/mo · Site+Run sheet £299+£69/mo (recommended) · Full system £399+£129/mo. No contract. Paul wants: a yes on Friday, recurring side income (he starts a Head of Content job 1 Aug 2026), software ownership retained, good standing with MJ.

Questions: (a) pricing structure/level; (b) pitch emphasis (no-shows vs growth vs credibility); (c) risks of being both worker and vendor; (d) what to say/do Friday; (e) what in the product bites later.

## Advisor responses

### The Contrarian
The localStorage single-phone architecture is the landmine, not a footnote. Dee's phone gets lost, dropped in a fryer, or replaced on a carrier upgrade and every crew list, shift history, and points record vanishes with zero backup. That's not a hypothetical — coordinators' phones fail constantly and Paul has no migration/export/backup story built. This will happen within the subscription's lifetime, and when it does, Paul — the vendor AND a driver who needs Dee to like him — eats the blame in person, not over a support ticket. Bigger problem: Paul is proposing an "auto-text next standby" midnight rule and a "points against" system that formalizes discipline against the exact coworkers he rides in the car with. He's asking to become the guy whose software puts a black mark on someone's record for cancelling — while still being a casual worker in the same WhatsApp thread. First time the algorithm dings someone unfairly (bad signal, wrong number saved, timezone/date confusion), it's Paul's app, Paul's fault, and Paul's carpool that gets awkward. Pricing is anchored fine against £5-15k agency quotes, but nobody has asked whether MJ's actual margins support £69-129/month recurring on top of setup — this is a small hospitality staffing agency, and "avoided cancellation pays 4 months" is Paul's framing, not tested against Dee's real cancellation rate. Nobody has addressed what happens contractually if Paul leaves this relationship, gets busy with the new agency job, or MJ wants to fire him as a driver but keep the software — is there any separation clause, or does everything collapse together because it's one person wearing three hats?

### The First Principles Thinker
The question everyone will answer is "is £299 the right number." Wrong question. Strip it back: what is Paul actually being asked to sell? MJ's owner isn't buying a website. He's buying relief from a specific pain he already quantified himself: £50-100 per no-show, chased manually on WhatsApp by one overworked coordinator. That is the entire deal. Everything else — the marketing site, testimonials, growth plan, 592 UK festivals — is Paul solving a problem MJ never stated. The owner asked for "a website" because that's the only vocabulary he has for "make my Dee-problem go away." He did not ask for a client-acquisition engine. So the first-principles move: the run sheet + midnight rule is the product. The marketing site is a proof-of-competence prop, not revenue. Pricing three tiers around "site vs site+tool vs full system" inverts the actual value order — it makes the free-to-build website the entry tier and buries the thing that pays for itself in an upsell. Rebuild the tiers around the no-show problem alone, and the growth stuff becomes a separate, later conversation once trust exists. Also worth naming directly: nobody asked whether MJ *should* grow. More clients means more coordination chaos for Dee before it means more margin — Paul is prescribing expansion to a business whose actual bottleneck is operational, not top-of-funnel. Fix the bottleneck first. Selling growth to a business that can't yet staff its current bookings reliably is solving the wrong constraint at the wrong time.

### The Expansionist
Everyone else on this council will fight over whether £69/mo is "fair" for a run sheet app. Wrong fight. Paul is pricing a coordinator tool when he's sitting on a staffing marketplace. Look at what MJ actually has: relationships with D&J and Sanjay's, a pool of Leicester workers, and — critically — the pain of matching bodies to shifts under time pressure. That's not a website problem. That's the problem Deputy, Rota, and every hospitality-staffing SaaS is solving for £billions of enterprise value. Paul's "run sheet" is a primitive version of a shift-marketplace product, and he's about to price it like a brochure add-on. The bigger number isn't MJ's £129/mo tier. It's this: D&J Catering runs Creamfields, Download, Trent Bridge, Donington, Warwick Castle — multiple concessionaires, multiple staffing agencies feeding them, all almost certainly running the exact same WhatsApp chaos MJ has. Paul isn't building MJ a website. He's building the first vertical shift-and-crew tool for UK festival/event catering staffing, and MJ is his unpaid beta customer with a live, painful, well-documented workflow to copy. So don't just sell tiers to MJ. Structure this so Paul retains IP and can white-label the run-sheet system to other staffing agencies feeding D&J or other concessionaires — Paul already has the receipts (real usage, real no-show savings) to pitch cold. That's the difference between a £129/mo side hustle and a multi-agency SaaS with one flagship reference customer he happens to already drive for.

### The Outsider
Okay, stranger-hearing-this-cold reaction: a guy who drives their van and works their shifts wants to sell them a £129/month "system" three days from now. My first thought isn't "smart hedge" — it's "wait, is Dee going to think Paul built a tool to make her job trackable by management, using data she generates?" That's the landmine nobody in this brief mentions: the run sheet logs shifts-per-person and a "points against confirm-then-cancellers" system. Paul is proposing to instrument his own coworkers. If Dee or the crew figure out the guy who car-shares with them built the no-show scoring tool, that's not a portfolio piece, that's a workplace relationship going cold fast. Second reaction as an outsider: the pricing means nothing without knowing what the owner currently pays for "the system" — which is £0, run by Dee on WhatsApp for free (her time aside). Every tier is a new cost the owner didn't have Thursday. £129/month sounds cheap to Paul because he's anchoring against agency quotes; the owner will anchor against "this cost nothing yesterday." Third: localStorage on one phone is a landmine phrased as a feature. That phone gets lost, dropped in a fryer, or replaced, and MJ's entire operational memory is gone — with Paul's name on it. To an outsider this reads less like "MVP" and more like "no backup, single point of failure, sold as a system." Fourth: no contract, cancel anytime, sounds trustworthy — until you realize it means MJ can extract three months of free labour-substitute value, cancel, and Paul's still driving their van next weekend with no leverage at all.

### The Executor
Monday morning first: Paul walks into that Zoom with a live URL, not a deck. That's already his biggest advantage — use it. Screen-share the run sheet, click through generating an actual WhatsApp message for the owner's own crew. Don't pitch a concept, demo a tool. That collapses the entire "can I trust this guy" question in ninety seconds. On pricing: don't lead with three tiers. Leading with choice creates hesitation on a Friday call with a non-technical small-business owner. Lead with tier 2 (£299/£69) as "here's what I built for you," demo it, THEN show tier 1 and 3 as "cheaper version" and "if you want the full no-show system later." Anchor down, not across. Cut the growth-plan/300-caterers pitch entirely for Friday. It's real value but it's a distraction from the yes you need this week. The owner cares about: does this stop no-shows costing me money, and can Dee actually use it Monday. Save NCASS/festival stats for a follow-up email after the yes, not the live call. Biggest execution risk: localStorage on one phone is a single point of failure with zero backup. If Dee's phone dies or she gets a new one, MJ loses their entire crew list and run history — during a festival season. Before Friday, add one thing: an export/backup button (even just to a Google Sheet or JSON download). That's a two-hour job, not a phase-2 promise. Don't let a demo crash on data loss be the story MJ tells about you. Ask for a start date and first payment on the call. Don't leave with "let me think about it."

## Chairman's verdict

### Where the Council Agrees
Three points landed independently across the board. First, localStorage on Dee's single phone is a live landmine, not an MVP quirk: Contrarian, Outsider, and Executor all said the same thing, that when the phone dies or gets upgraded, MJ's entire operational memory vanishes with Paul's name on it, in person, while he still needs the driving work. Second, the growth plan is the wrong pitch for Friday: First Principles and Executor both cut it, and Contrarian's margin question supports them. MJ's stated pain is no-shows at £50-100 each; the 300-caterer target list solves a problem the owner never raised. Third, the worker/vendor collision is real and social, not contractual: Paul is proposing a points system that scores the coworkers he carpools with, and the first wrong ding lands on him personally.

### Where the Council Clashes
The genuine fight is what the product is. Executor says keep the tiers, lead with tier 2 as a demo, anchor down. First Principles says the tiers are inverted: the run sheet plus midnight rule is the value, the website is a competence prop, so rebuild pricing around the no-show problem alone. Both are defensible: Executor optimizes for a clean yes in three days with materials that already exist; First Principles optimizes for pricing what actually pays for itself. The Expansionist stands alone arguing this is a multi-agency SaaS play into D&J's supply chain. All peer reviews named this the biggest blind spot: Paul does not own the D&J relationship, MJ does, and floating "I'll sell this to others" on Friday reads as being used as a guinea pig. But the kernel survives: retaining IP for future white-labelling matches Paul's stated goals. Wrong pitch, right structure.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught
Peer review surfaced four things no single advisor had. Dee is the real decision-maker: her informal WhatsApp control is exactly what gets automated away, and demoing a scoring tool in front of her could trigger the coworker problem live on the call. The downside scenario nobody planned for: the owner says "just give me the site free," and Paul, employee and friend, has no scripted refusal. "No contract, cancel anytime" cuts both ways: MJ can extract months of value then cancel while Paul still drives their van, and it is also Paul's own exit ramp when the agency job starts. And nobody war-gamed Monday: an awkward no on Friday and Paul is still in that WhatsApp group at the weekend.

### The Recommendation
Sell the run sheet, not the bundle. Reframe Friday around one sentence: "You told me a no-show costs £50-100; here's the tool that stops them, live, with Dee's exact message format built in." Demo it on screen with real crew data. Present tier 2 as "what I built for you" (Executor's anchoring), keep the site as included proof of polish, and drop the growth plan entirely into a post-yes follow-up email. Before the call, defuse the two landmines: ship a backup/export function so data loss cannot become the story, and reposition the points system as "your policy, configurable by you," so the owner owns the discipline and Paul owns the software. Bring one page of written terms separating the software relationship from the driving work, so either can end without killing the other. Have a scripted answer for "do it free": "The free version was the demo; the £69 is what keeps it running and backed up." Ask for a start date on the call. Do not pitch Dee's replacement; pitch Dee's relief, and ideally show her the tool before the owner sees it.

### The One Thing to Do First
Tonight, build the export/backup button: one click, crew list and shift history out to a Google Sheet or JSON file. It is a two-hour job, it kills the unanimous number-one risk before it can kill the deal, and it converts "localStorage on one phone" from a confession into a feature called "your data, backed up, yours."
